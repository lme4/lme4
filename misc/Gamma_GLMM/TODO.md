* GH #936 log-gaussian `sigma` bias: mechanism precisely characterized (glmer's moment-based phi is missing a degrees-of-freedom correction) and now **implemented, generalized, and on by default**: `glmerControl(disp_dof_correction=TRUE)` (now the default; `FALSE` reproduces the old uncorrected behaviour), wired through `mkGlmerDevfun()`/`mkdevfun()`/`glmerPwrssUpdate()`/`glmerLaplaceHandle()` down to `profilePhi()` in `external.cpp`. `qEff` is computed once per fit (doesn't depend on theta/phi/beta) in R (`computeQEff()`, `R/modular.R`) as the actual rank of the combined fixed+random "saturated" design `[X, Z]`, via `Matrix::rankMatrix(cbind(X, t(Zt)), method="qr")` -- the classical `RSS/(n-p)` logic, generalized, and confirmed (via a `poly(x,3)` fixed-effect addition on top of several RE structures) to correctly catch redundancy *between* fixed and random effects too, not just within the RE structure. Validated on the real GH #936 example (`nlme::Rail`): `disp_dof_correction=TRUE` moves glmer's sigma from 3.29 to 4.04, matching glmmTMB/mgcv/glmmPQL (~4.02). Two real bugs found and fixed while validating the default flip: (1) `computeQEff()`'s rank computation could fail outright for very large `q` (observation-level random effects, `q~n`) -- `"QR factorization ... out of memory"` at `q~50000` -- now caught via `tryCatch`, falling back to no correction with a warning; (2) `computeQEff()` was running unconditionally regardless of family, even though `profilePhi()` only ever uses the result for free-dispersion families -- now gated on `hasNoScale(family)` (same check `mkMerMod()` uses), so fixed-dispersion fits (Poisson, binomial, negative.binomial) skip the computation entirely rather than wasting it (this was also what made the OOM case above expensive for no reason). Regression tests in `tests/testthat/test-sigma-dof.R` (sigma-ratio checks across single-scalar/random-slope/crossed-intercept/crossed-slopes-sharing-a-covariate/nested structures, plus the `poly(x,3)` fixed/random-redundancy check and a default-value check).
